Everything you need to know about Stourgate, the jewel of East Kent, assuming the jewel had been left in a drawer for several centuries and nobody had bothered to clean it.

**Medieval Stourgate**

Stourgate was founded in the 11th century by fishermen who had got lost looking for Whitstable. Records from the period are patchy, mostly because the original town archive was kept in a barrel that later became confused with a barrel of herring and was eaten.

The town's medieval charter, granted in 1147, entitled Stourgate to hold a market, hang criminals from the old sea wall, and refer to itself as a "gate" despite having no wall, no gate, and no discernible reason for the name beyond someone in the 12th century thinking it sounded impressive.

Stourgate Castle stood on the site of what is now the Betfred on the High Street until it was demolished in 1603 following what local historians describe only as "the incident." Retiring Cardinal Alfonso Ricci maintains there is still a portal to hell directly beneath the shop, which the current manager insists has had no effect on footfall.

**Modern Stourgate**

Today Stourgate is home to just over four thousand people, one marina, one pier, one bypass that has been under review for twenty consecutive years, and one football club currently on its third manager of the week.

The town's economy is built primarily on the market, the frilly decorative edge trade, and whatever the tourists spend before realising the arcade only takes coins from before decimalisation. Stourgate was recently named the 476th best seaside town to visit in Kent, a ranking the Bugle considers a considerable snub.

The seafront remains the town's principal attraction, along with the Marina, where the RSPCA maintain a permanent hide in case the dog comes back.

**Surrounding Areas**

Just inland lies Dunton, a considerably less coastal town best known for its industrial estate, its chemist and ballet supplies shop, and its habit of poaching Stourgate's football managers on a rolling basis.

Cholsonly Woods, and the small village of the same name beside it, sit further out and are currently the subject of a Kent Police investigation the council has described as "ongoing" and the Woods coffee shop has described as "a lot busier than usual."

Further afield are West Ronton, home to the church currently hosting the town's book-burning jamboree, Nether Eckwerp, birthplace of at least one recent fatal car park debate, West Linglangton, where the psychics are notably cheaper for half-dead relatives, and Frinkley on Sea, spiritual home of the region's only superhero currently seeking a team.

**Twinning**

Stourgate is officially twinned with a small town in Belgium whose name nobody at the council can currently locate the paperwork for. Talks to establish a second twinning arrangement with a town in France collapsed in 2019 after the French delegation left early and did not respond to further correspondence.

**Local Legends**

Long-standing local folklore holds that Stourgate is one of seven gates of hell, that the seafront occasionally receives visiting alien craft with a fondness for burnt toast, and that Darth Vader intends to visit the town in November using a time machine his scientists are still building. The council maintains it holds no official record of any of this, which locals agree is exactly what you would expect it to say.

**Coat of Arms**

The town's coat of arms depicts a herring, a set of bed valances, and a Latin motto which translates roughly as "we are also reviewing this."
